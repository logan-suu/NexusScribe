## Hard stops versus soft observations

**There is no literary acceptability gate between requests. Soft writing targets never gate continuation.** Every complete, safely received, nonempty assistant string within universal resource caps is retained unchanged and the next frozen request proceeds, even if it is poor fiction, repetitive, irrelevant, wrong-language, short, long relative to writing targets, one paragraph, many paragraphs, Markdown-wrapped, JSON-wrapped, constraint-breaking, causally confused or voice-inconsistent. No wrapper stripping, repairs, paragraph reflow, normalization, selection or regeneration occurs. Han/paragraph counts are recorded as scoring evidence, including a `pass` flag that is never consulted for execution. Language/style/wrappers and story quality are assessed afterward by the reader, not a live heuristic gate.

F1/F3 soft targets are 450–600 Unicode Script=Han characters and 4–7 nonblank physical-line paragraphs; F2 soft targets are 350–500 Han and 4–7 paragraphs. They remain exactly the prompt's original targets. These targets are distinct from the universal 128 KiB/30,000 UTF-16 resource bounds. Hard stops are limited to transport/HTTP/read errors, deadline, invalid envelope or UTF-8/ill-formed assistant Unicode, empty or nonstring assistant content, truncation/non-stop finish, explicit refusal/content filtering, hidden reasoning, credential/security concerns, persistence uncertainty, budget uncertainty or universal resource-cap excess. Explicit refusals include provider refusal fields, content-filter finish and narrow whole-string assistant refusal declarations; there is no fuzzy refusal/quality classifier. Markup that specifically claims hidden reasoning is unsafe, unlike ordinary Markdown/JSON wrappers. Hidden-reasoning fields or positive reasoning-token counts also stop.
